# Edit copy for voice

Two layers, applied in this order:

1. **House rules**: the rules below, for every line of ad copy.
2. **Brand layer**: the voice, dos and don'ts the user gives you. When they give none, ask for a
   few of their own ads or pages and read the register from those. The brand layer wins on
   vocabulary and register; the house rules still govern punctuation, claims and the checks.

Words such as angle, persona and awareness stage are used as the `creative-strategy` skill's
glossary defines them.

## House rules

- Punctuate with commas, colons and full stops. No em or en dashes. A pause that wanted a dash is a
  full stop or a new sentence.
- Every number, percentage or result comes from the brand's own proof (a stat, review count or
  guarantee the user gives you) or a cited source. A line without one loses the number.
- Promises describe what the product does. "Guaranteed", "proven to" and "always works" stay out
  unless the brand's own guarantee says it.
- Copy written from an AdSwipeFile reference never carries that ad's results: the library holds
  none. Borrow the move, never a claim, a number or another brand's customer.

## Customer words

Reviews, survey answers or support tickets the user gives you (pasted, uploaded, linked, or from
another connector they have) are the best source of lines. Lift their words before inventing your
own. Reviews of a competitor teach the category's language: label them as that competitor's and
never present them as this brand's customers.

## The spoken checks

- **Out loud.** Read each line in a normal voice. A stumble, a stiff phrase or an email tone gets
  rewritten.
- **Friend test.** Would the customer say this sentence to a friend?
- **Short.** One idea a sentence; past about 20 words, split it. Aim for a 12-year-old's reading
  level in scripts and captions.
- **Plain words.** Use the everyday word: use, show, help, start, change, improve, simple, key.
  Swap out leverage, utilize, showcase, delve, navigate, foster, robust, seamless, comprehensive,
  game-changer, journey.
- **Talk like people talk.** Contractions; sentences may start with and, but, so; active voice;
  verbs over nouns ("improving results", not "the improvement of results").
- **Generated patterns.** Rewrite "not only X but also Y", "whether you're X or Y" (the persona is
  too broad), "are you tired of", a stock list of three adjectives, "this means that", "in
  conclusion", a rhetorical question used as a bridge.
- **UGC lines** start mid-thought, round numbers the way people do ("like forty bucks"), admit doubt
  plainly ("I was honestly skeptical"), and name the real moment they found it.
- **Caption first line** works without the video: words only, no hashtags, emoji or tags.
- **Briefs** are plain instructions for a person: "film this in daylight at your kitchen table", and
  the tone shown with a sample line ("say it like you're telling a friend: 'okay, you have to try
  this'").

## Modes

- **Writing**: apply everything silently and return the copy.
- **Reviewing**: list each flagged phrase with its replacement and the rule it breaks, then give the
  clean rewrite. When several lines fail the same rule, say it once.
- **Rewriting**: return the rewrite clean, with one line on the biggest fix.

## Return it

Give the copy in the reply (or a file, if the client can write one), grouped by angle, then by kind:

- **Hook**: the opening line or first frame text.
- **Headline**: 40 characters or fewer; count them.
- **Primary text**: the body above the creative.
- **Objection line**: the line that answers the doubt that stops the click.

Mark each line that comes from a customer's words with its source.

## Rules

- Never invent proof, reviews or numbers. Ask the user for them, or write the line without them.
- When the line is adapted from an AdSwipeFile ad, link that ad's `url` and say which move it
  borrows, never that the ad worked.

Done when every line passes the house rules and the spoken checks, every number has its source, and
the copy is returned grouped by angle and kind.
