# Write a short-form script

A script is a transcript of someone talking, cut so every word earns its second, with the picture
carrying what the words don't have to say. The speaker's or brand's voice, dos and don'ts set the
register; they come from the user. Voice and claim checks: `edit-copy-for-voice`. Words are used as the `creative-strategy` skill's glossary
defines them.

## Inputs

- The concept: the brief, or the offer statement, persona, trigger moment, mechanic, format and
  hook. A script with no concept gets one first (`generate-ad-concepts`).
- The speaker's own words: notes, a voice memo transcript, a draft, from the user. Their sentences
  are the script, tightened; you write new lines only where they left a gap.
- A reference ad, when the hook is borrowed: `get_ad` and its `breakdown`. The `units` give each
  scene with what it shows, the words on screen and what is said in the first 3 seconds; `remix` is
  a fill-in script from its structure. Use the opening, not a copy of the ad.

## The rules

1. **Spoken, whole sentences.** Each line sounds like a person talking. A teaser plus a punchline
   fragment, or a list of fragments, becomes one natural sentence.
2. **A question is a turn.** It moves into the next beat and is answered in full, never by one word.
3. **Cut what costs nothing to lose.** Read each line without its extra words; if nothing is lost, cut
   them.
4. **The visual carries context.** Say the point once and let the screen show the detail. Cues sit on
   their own line in brackets: `[three creators' "I tried it" videos pop up]`. A long list: say three
   or four, show the rest as text.
5. **Everything is sayable.** Parentheses, slashes and asides become their own sentence.
6. **Keep the speaker's point.** Tighten the wording, never swap the idea. A likely dictation slip:
   fix the words, keep the meaning, say what you changed.
7. **Show the product working.** The speaker uses it on camera with one short line riding the action,
   in place of a separate explainer.
8. **A borrowed hook keeps its situation.** Name the story that made the reference believable (who
   the speaker is, what just happened), and carry that story into ours; the words follow from it.
9. **The CTA matches reality.** "Link below" only when there is a link; the product's name the way
   the speaker would say it.
10. **Literal over clever.** Each line says what actually happens. Wordplay on the product and
    figures of speech read as a slogan.
11. **Land the point plainly.** A beat ends on what changes for the viewer, in ordinary words.
12. **The first frame works muted.** One persistent on-screen line in the first three seconds carries
    the premise for sound-off viewers; captions alone don't.

**Breakdown videos** (explaining an ad to people who make ads): each point opens by naming the move,
then why it makes people believe or buy, then how to use it. The ad's viewer is "someone" or "people";
"you" is the marketer watching. A pattern across several ads stays brand-neutral in the spoken line
and shows the example on screen. Fit the runtime by cutting whole beats, never the reasoning; when it
still runs long, give the real runtime and offer a split into two videos. Results of the ads
explained are never stated: library ads were kept running, which is why they are worth studying.

## How to answer

1. The revised section first, in a quote block, cues on their own lines.
2. The changes, numbered, each with its reason in one sentence.
3. When the script is complete: estimated runtime (about 2.5 spoken words a second) and where it
   could be shorter.

Long scripts in pieces: review one piece at a time, then ask for the next.

## The finished script

Give it in the reply (or a file, if the client can write files) as a beat table, one row per beat:

| From (s) | To (s) | Voiceover | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 3 | the line, verbatim | the persistent premise line and the cues |

Above it: the format, the ratio 9:16 and the maximum length in seconds. Below it: the reference
ads by `url`, if a hook was borrowed.

Done when every line passes the rules, every cue is marked, the runtime is stated, and the beat
table is in the reply.

## Rules

- Never state a reference ad's results: spend, ROAS, conversions, or that it beat another.
- Never invent lines a reference ad does not have; when its breakdown comes without units, work
  from what came and pass its note on once.
- Claims and numbers in the script come from the user's proof points.
